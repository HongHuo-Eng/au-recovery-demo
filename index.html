    Using this workspace

    Instructions

    Use real response data to understand relationships, compare explicit preferences and plan validation. Suggestions remain hypotheses until new experiments test them.

    A short first pass

    1. Inspect a response. Select a case and compare Reported observations only with GP, interpolation and baseline methods.
    2. Check validation. Read the holdout errors and study limitations before interpreting a smooth curve as predictive evidence.
    3. Explore trade-offs. For eligible controls, set meaningful constraints and preferences. Compare the selected candidate with measured settings.
    4. Draft a test. Add a candidate to the memo, review factor levels and fixed conditions, then generate a proposed experiment matrix.
    Response cases are separate; not every horizontal axis is a control

    Each relationship applies to its own source series and conditions. Material composition, pH, dosage and residual concentration do not become a common training table simply because they appear in this workspace.

    The residual-concentration relation describes capacity versus reported c_rest or Ce. Ce is an outcome coordinate, not a freely set initial concentration C0. Inspecting that curve does not recommend an initial concentration or establish a recovery percentage. Descriptive cases do not enter operational candidate selection.

    Models, uncertainty bands and holdout errors

    Models were trained offline on CPU. Browser controls select saved predictions and evaluate preferences; they do not retrain the models. GP uncertainty depends on its kernel and noise assumptions. It is not independently calibrated experimental uncertainty, and source error bars may have unspecified statistics.

    MAE is average absolute prediction error; RMSE gives larger errors more weight. Compare them only within the same response and units. Whole-condition holdout keeps related records together; endpoint holdouts extrapolate relative to their training range. A strong retrospective score does not establish performance on new batches. Baselines do not inherit GP standard deviations.

    Constraints, Pareto trade-offs and weighted ranking

    Constraints define which evaluated points are acceptable. A target percentage refers to the highest observed response, not gold recovery. For the dosage case, lower reported Au and retention of reported Cu are proxy objectives, not a measure of recovered-product purity.

    Pareto candidates represent objective trade-offs: improving one objective may worsen another. Weights express your priorities when ranking candidates; they do not change measured evidence. A lower material ratio is not automatically an economic saving. Screening conservatism uses the chosen model-SD multiple without providing a calibrated probability guarantee.

    Weights can move the selected candidate away from the lowest feasible input. That is a preference ranking, not evidence of an experimentally superior condition. The reported pH axis may describe preparation or material pH; distance from a preferred pH is not acid consumption. Reported potential magnitude is not cell voltage or energy use.

    No feasible candidate is a valid outcome. The result depends on the displayed range, constraints, model and preferences; it does not prove that the chemistry is impossible.

    The experiment matrix is a proposal, not a joint model

    The default three ratios and three doses form nine combinations. Three blocks give 27 treatments, plus one no-adsorbent control per block, for 30 planned treatments when pH is fixed. Adding optional solution-pH levels proposes an additional factor and changes the matrix size.

    Proposed solution-pH settings require their own justification; existing pH relationships do not automatically validate them. Record batches, preparation, volume, contact time, temperature and fixed or varied pH. A complete form still requires protocol review. Model outputs are not multiplied into a joint performance prediction, and no planned row is an executed experiment.

    The memo and settings belong to the current page session. Refreshing the page resets that session; the presentation does not offer a plan export.

    What the presentation establishes and what viewers can access

    Observed values, model estimates and proposed experiments are different evidence states. Missing matched initial concentrations, dilution and mass-balance information prevent interpreting a lower liquid-phase Au value as metallic-gold yield. External validation requires new, independent experiments.

    No source-file, report or data downloads are offered. This does not keep chart values confidential: the browser receives the numbers needed to render the presentation, and viewers can inspect or save them. Original research attachments are not included.

    Real observations, model estimates and proposed tests are distinguished throughout. No joint global performance model or experimentally validated improvement is claimed.
